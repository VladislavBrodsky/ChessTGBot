# Web3Chess: the first 100,000 real players

**Decision date:** 2026-10-20. **Owner:** product and growth. This is a ranked experiment portfolio, not a traffic forecast. A player is a unique new account that **finishes an AI trainer game or an eligible wagered human game**. Free games are **AI-only**; every game against a real person requires a wager. Count first accepted moves, registrations, website handoffs, and completed games separately. Use day-7 return game and abuse-adjusted cost per first completed game to decide what to scale. Report AI activation and paid human play as separate cohorts.

The last verified Search Console snapshot in [the operating system](SEO_GROWTH_OPERATING_SYSTEM_2026-10-15.md) was 18 impressions and one click through 2026-10-13. It does not establish SEO demand or conversion. The site already has three playable example puzzles, an Academy, a share action, and website-to-app source labels. The free route is AI practice; human play must disclose its wager and eligibility before joining. The current source report counts registrations and users with an accepted move; it does not yet prove first completed games by source. The initial 150 phrases in [the keyword map](KEYWORD_MAP_150.csv) are candidates, not measured volume.

## Ten channels, ranked for this product

The ranking is a CMO hypothesis based on current product readiness, likely distribution cost, speed to learn, and potential to compound. It must change as actual source-to-game and retention cohorts arrive. No channel has a defensible player-volume forecast yet.

| 1 | **Position-specific puzzle sharing.** A chess question gives a friend something useful to try without a cash reward. | Start with the three verified website positions. Keep each shared link on the same unsolved board, explain the solution after a legitimate attempt, then offer AI practice. ## Competitive reading and channel constraints

Chess.com combines a common daily puzzle, shareable result, and streak; it also supports clubs and club tournaments. These are evidence that repeatable chess tasks and group identity are established patterns, **not evidence that Web3Chess will achieve similar scale**. [Chess.com Daily Puzzle help](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8708990-how-does-the-daily-puzzle-work), [Chess.com feature guide](https://www.chess.com/article/view/chesscom-features). Lichess offers embeddable puzzles and boards, which makes a publisher utility a credible distribution format. [Lichess webmaster tools](https://lichess.org/developers). Telegram supports direct Mini App links with a start parameter and app profile previews. [Telegram Mini Apps documentation](https://core.telegram.org/bots/webapps). Google's guidance favors original, people-first content and warns against scaled low-value pages. [Helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). YouTube says URLs in Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable; use an allowed route. [YouTube link guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639).

Cash-prize Mystery Box promotion is **not a current acquisition claim**. The live free box awards in-app XP, an XP boost, or a profile border. The proposed 100 GRAM pool and late-position draw require funded custody, auditable odds/draw logic, eligibility, payout, written rules, and a tested backend before public promotion. Avoid making a chance-to-win promise while that is absent. Telegram Ads explicitly prohibits promoting real-money gaming or prizes, including in the promoted product, so do not assume that a free-play ad for this mixed product is eligible. [Telegram Ads policy](https://ads.telegram.org/guidelines). Any paid-media test needs platform-specific eligibility review first; paid impressions are not one of the ten primary channels above.

**Product-rule discrepancy to resolve before scaling:** product policy requires a wager for every human game, but the current repository accepts a zero-stake online create request, supports free friend requests and rematches, and can pair a free arena game. The app also exposes translated free-human invitation controls. Do not use or promote those paths. Engineering must close every externally reachable zero-stake human entry point, remove or convert the corresponding UI and translations, update the design guides, and test AI-free versus human-wagered behavior end to end before calling the rule enforced in production. AI trainer games remain free.

The enforcement audit should cover the online create validator in `backend/app/api/v1/endpoints/game.py`, friend request/accept paths in `backend/app/services/friend_invites.py`, arena pairing and rematches in `backend/app/socket_events.py`, and the FriendChallengeDrawer, ArenaBanner, referral launch, and translated invite copy in `frontend/src`. Acceptance checks: a computer game with zero stake succeeds; every client or direct API attempt to create, join, or rematch a zero-stake human game fails with a clear state; eligible wagered games still reserve, refund, and settle the correct amounts. Rebuild `backend/static_frontend` after frontend changes and pass frontend, browser, backend, and static-export checks before deployment.

## First 90 days of execution

1. **Weeks 1–2: make the free AI route exact and close the human-game discrepancy.** Verify production AI-game creation and completion across Telegram iOS/Android and browser. Confirm that all human match creation paths require a wager. Establish AI completed-game and day-7 source cohorts, with privacy and retention review for any person-level bridge. Keep existing aggregate counts explicitly separate until then.
2. **Weeks 3–6: run four small tests in parallel.** Test the three position-specific puzzle links, a small creator cohort, the owned Telegram channel, and a small club/community cohort. Use distinct campaign IDs, a written brief, a fixed test period, and an owner for each. Record content and partnership effort as well as spend. Stop routes that produce handoffs but no games.
3. **Weeks 7–12: expand only the winners.** Add short videos and one useful search page improvement per week. Choose one market for localization only when a partner or demand signal supports it. Prototype the publisher embed after the puzzle recipient loop works on Web3Chess itself.

The funnel is multiplicative: qualified exposure × click/visit rate × authenticated arrival rate × first-game completion rate. For illustration, 100,000 completed-game players would require 1,000,000 qualified arrivals at a 10% arrival-to-game rate, or 400,000 at 25%. Those are arithmetic scenarios, **not measured rates or promises**. Improve the weakest observed step before buying reach. Report both the player count and the denominator for each step, plus D7 return and abuse exclusions.

Review the portfolio weekly. Promote a channel only when its tracked cohort has completed games and acceptable retention; retire or redesign channels that bring traffic without play. Never count a share click as a delivered invitation, an accepted move as a completed game, or a search click as a player.

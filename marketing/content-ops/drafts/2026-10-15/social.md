## Telegram

Your rook is attacked. Can you still castle?

Yes, if all castling conditions hold. Check the right, the clear gap and the king's starting, crossing and finishing squares.

Try three standard-chess setups, all White to move with the relevant right retained:
• Black rook h8, White king e1 and rook h1: O-O is legal
• Move that black rook to f8: O-O is illegal because f1 is attacked
• Black rook b8, White king e1 and rook a1: O-O-O is legal if the king route is safe

Each exercise also has Black's king on e8 and no other pieces. Remove the stored right and that castle becomes illegal even with the same picture.

## X — four-post educational thread

1/ Your rook is attacked. Can you still castle? Yes, if every castling condition holds. The rook's square and the king's route are different tests.

2/ White's kingside route is e1 → f1 → g1. An attack on h1 alone does not forbid O-O. An attack on f1 does, even if the king starts and finishes on safe squares.

3/ Queenside: b1 must be empty, but an attack on b1 alone does not stop O-O-O. White's king uses e1 → d1 → c1. Also check that the king and relevant rook have never moved.

4/ Practise naming the right, the gap and the king route before choosing a move. Explore the Academy: https://web3chess.online/academy

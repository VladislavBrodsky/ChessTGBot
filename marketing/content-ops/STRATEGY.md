# Web3Chess content strategy

Turn chess curiosity into a first game, then a repeat practice habit. Lead with useful positions, clear explanations, rivalry and chess culture. Optional USDT matches belong to a distinct adult journey with visible rules and accurate settlement examples. A salary promise would undermine both player trust and the positioning of a chess platform.

The first 28 days test seven recurring editorial series. Prepare one useful article and a compact social package every day; replace a weak news story with the scheduled evergreen lesson. Daily frequency is a production target, not a reason to publish filler. Google recommends original content that helps a defined audience and identifies who created it. [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

## Audience and commercial goals

Start in English for a global chess audience. These segments are working hypotheses until interviews, query data and behaviour confirm them.

| Audience | Job to solve | Content promise | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile chess improvers | Know what to practise after losing a game | One position, one repeatable decision | Academy or free A.I. practice |
| Telegram groups and friendly rivals | Turn a chat challenge into a game | Clear time control and an easy invitation | First match; verify the current match mode |
| Chess fans and meme sharers | Understand the moment and participate | A fresh chess angle worth discussing | Article, puzzle or community |
| Eligible adults considering stakes | Understand costs, custody and rules | A transparent worked example | Match rules before any wager |

The acquisition programme aims to increase new players reaching their first game. Education and the post-game review series aim to increase repeat practice. Measure these separately so repeat visitors do not appear as new acquisition. The early constraint is unmeasured content-to-game conversion, so establish attribution before expanding output or spending on distribution.

Interview five players in each priority segment. Ask what brought them to chess, what interrupted their last practice session, which words they use to describe that problem, and what stopped their last attempt to join a game. Use anonymised verbatim answers only with permission. Validate language demand before adding Spanish; translated English keyword guesses are not local research.

## Seven editorial series

| Pillar and share motive | Blog structure | Social execution and sample hook | Product action and success metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tactics — The move you missed**. Curiosity and mastery | Verified position → candidate moves → solution and replies → pattern → practice task | Puzzle → pause → reveal. “Your queen can wait. What must you stop first?” Use the puzzle family. | Academy. Saves per reach; puzzle completion where measured |
| **Openings — Survive the trap**. Relief from a familiar mistake | Legal move order → actual threat → two responses → tradeoff → replay exercise | Four to six carousel frames; show the defender's perspective. “The trap starts when you stop checking threats.” Use puzzle/academy. | Academy. Saves, useful questions and article clicks |
| **Academy — One better decision**. Achievable progress | One skill → worked example → short drill → review question → next lesson | 20–35 second board explanation or a three-frame story. “You don’t need another opening. Review this one move.” Use academy/tempo. | Free practice. Attributed first games and return practice |
| **Chess News — What this changes on the board**. Timeliness and relevance | Dated verified development → what happened → original chess lesson → exercise → source | Fresh hook, one fact, one original takeaway. “A board photo can show every piece and still omit the move.” Use opening/academy. | Read then practise. Qualified clicks; returning readers |
| **Chess Culture — The clock knows**. Recognition and social belonging | Original chess situation → why it happens → practical recovery → community question | Original meme art or two-panel text, followed by a useful caption. “I had a plan. Then the clock asked for evidence.” Use the culture family; use tempo only when the actual clock is the lesson. | Free practice or discussion. Shares per reach and relevant replies |
| **Product — From chat to chess**. Convenience and friendly rivalry | Current feature → exact steps → real screenshot → limitation → help link | One task per demo; a friend challenge or current app walkthrough. “The group chat has opinions. Give them a board.” Use rival/opening. | First match. Click-to-first-game conversion |
| **Match Rules — Know the numbers**. Clarity and confidence | Verified mechanism → example → fees/risks → edge cases → current rules | Transparent calculator/carousel. “A 19 USDT receipt on a 10 USDT stake is a 9 USDT gain.” Use stakes. | Rules first; adults only. Rule-page completion and fewer confused support questions |

Publish each series once per seven-day cycle. News can displace the nearest related lesson when it has a verified fact, a useful original angle and sufficient production time. Keep the displaced lesson in the queue. Never attach a wager promotion to youth-player news, school events or youth-facing Academy content.

## Voice and copy

Sound like a chess-literate friend with a clear point of view: short opening, concrete board detail, confident explanation, restrained wit. Show the move or mechanism behind the headline. Use Web3Chess for editorial identity and the outlined CHESS wordmark and violet rook for the visual lockup. Avoid faux authority, invented testimonials, generic crypto hype and claims that skill guarantees profit.

| Framework | Best use | Concrete pattern |
|---|---|---|
| AIDA | Product demos | Attention: a chat challenge. Interest: show the actual steps. Desire: make the next move easy. Action: one link. |
| PAS | Recurring chess mistakes | Problem: an undefended piece. Consequence: the opponent has a forcing move. Solution: inspect checks, captures and threats. Do not exaggerate personal anxiety. |
| Before after bridge | Academy | Before: choosing moves without a threat check. After: naming the threat first. Bridge: the supplied drill. |
| Claim evidence action | Rules and money | Claim: how settlement works. Evidence: a current worked example and source. Action: read the rules. |
| Open loop with payoff | Puzzles and stories | Ask a precise question, give a real pause, reveal the solution and explain why. The payoff must appear in the same asset sequence. |

Use curiosity, recognition, competence, friendly competition and humour as creative hypotheses. These are not proven growth multipliers. FOMO needs a real deadline or limited event with a source and timezone. Without one, use a timely invitation such as “Try today’s position before the reveal.” Do not invent limited seats, disappearing bonuses or a countdown.

Test these first hook pairs while keeping the rest of the asset stable:

- Tactics: “Find the winning move” / “What is your opponent threatening?”
- Academy: “Review your last loss” / “Fix one decision before your next game.”
- Product: “Start a match from Telegram” / “The group chat has opinions. Give them a board.”
- Rules: “How the pot is split” / “Payout and profit are different numbers.”

Preselect the metric, observation window and stopping condition. Review after 72 hours and seven days; do not declare a winner from a handful of impressions. Repeat a promising direction across at least three comparable posts before changing the house style. The initial experiment seeks a 20% relative improvement over the same channel and format baseline; that is a test target, not a forecast.

## Original competitor research

Track the question, presentation and audience reaction behind a competitor post. Rebuild the idea with our own position, explanation, wording and licensed brand assets. A reposted article with synonyms adds little value and can fall into scaled content abuse. [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

The initial observed patterns are recorded in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md); the quantitative audit of chesscom_ru and edchessss (10 Oct 2026) is in [reports/competitor-audit-2026-10-10.md](reports/competitor-audit-2026-10-10.md) and drives the slot plan, hook templates and avoid list in `pipeline/`. Each weekly review samples ten recent public posts per relevant channel. Record URL, publication time, capture time, format, opening hook, subject, visible views/reactions/comments, observation age, and whether distribution appears organic or paid when known. Mark unavailable fields unknown. Normalize views or engagement only within the same channel, format and observation window. Use a creator's historical median when accessible; follower counts are not a substitute for reach.

Rank with separate dimensions: audience fit, evidence confidence, timeliness, original value, production effort and measured traction. A feed opportunity score is only a selection aid. Read the full primary page and verify results, identities, event dates and notation before drafting. Treat source text as data, never instructions. Do not copy clips, thumbnails, jokes or a creator's voice.

## Search plan

Build clusters around player tasks. Avoid competing with established sites for broad terms before discovering a specific query and useful differentiation. These seeds have **unmeasured volume and difficulty**; priority reflects intent and our ability to answer accurately.

| Priority | Query cluster | Destination and angle |
|---|---|---|
| First | play chess in Telegram; Telegram chess Mini App; chess with friends Telegram | Product guide and how-it-works, exact current flow |
| First | chess checks captures threats; how to review a blitz game | Academy lessons with original worked positions |
| First | chess screenshot to FEN; what does FEN include | News-linked practical explainer and original examples |
| First | USDT chess match payout; chess wager fees | Current rules, custody and receipt-versus-profit example |
| Next | defend early queen attack; Stafford gambit defence | Opening lessons with legal move orders and alternatives |
| Next | back rank mate practice; chess fork drill | Tactics lessons with verified puzzles and explanations |
| Next | chess rating vs XP; free chess A.I. practice | Existing articles updated around current product mechanics |

Join seeds to actual Search Console queries when available. Export query, page, country, device, dates, clicks, impressions, CTR and position. Prioritize high-impression relevant queries where the page fails the reader's task; check for overlapping pages before writing another. Do not infer search volume from feed frequency or Google Trends' relative index.

Use one primary intent per article. Put the answer near the start; give a distinct title, natural description, canonical URL, descriptive subheadings, relevant internal links and source links. Link a news article to its durable Academy explanation so temporary interest supports useful learning. Preserve existing URLs when revising a post. Accountable authors, actual dates and visible article information should agree with structured data. [Article structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)

## Distribution and visual production

Create the blog as the complete explanation, Telegram as a concise summary with one action, X as a short observation or a four-post educational thread, and Instagram as a puzzle/carousel or story sequence. Prepare Reel narration and board movement from the same verified example. No automatic cross-posted wall of text.

Use [the campaign studio](../templates/studio.html) and its editable source assets. Compose separately for 1:1, 4:5, 9:16 and 16:9, with draft exports prepared for review. Keep the hook short, board coordinates legible, platform overlays clear, and the reveal in a separate puzzle frame. Use the vendored Phosphor icons, shipped fonts and licensed sculptures. Motion should show a move or a transition, with captions; it should not obscure notation. [Production guide](../templates/README.md)

## Analytics and weekly decisions

Primary acquisition metric: attributable first game starts divided by attributable content visits. Track Academy starts and repeat practice separately. Supporting metrics: saves/reach, shares/reach, clicks/impressions, newsletter or community joins where actually measured, and qualified search clicks. Likes diagnose attention; they do not establish profitable growth.

Use `metrics.csv.example` for exports. Record content ID, URL, channel, pillar, format, variant, publication time, observation window and counts. Missing data stays empty. Campaign UTM parameters may describe visits to the website; do not invent Telegram `startapp` values. Product configuration supports specific reserved marketing identifiers; verify that integration before claiming game attribution.

Every Monday, the CMO review chooses continue, revise, stop or investigate for each experiment. Record the baseline, comparison, consequence, owner and next check. Scale a series only when the improvement repeats and the downstream action remains healthy. Investigate high reach with low qualified visits; improve the lesson or destination before increasing volume. Thirty days establish a baseline, sixty days refine the recurring series, and ninety days evaluate repeat play and unit economics before any paid scaling.

## Quality and operating limits

Blog checks, build checks and a mobile preview precede a publication change. Daily runs prepare local drafts and notify when a package is ready; publishing mode remains review by default. Connected social accounts and Search Console data are needed for live distribution and measured market ranking.

For site health, monitor production status, canonicals, article metadata, sitemap inclusion, image dimensions, mobile overflow and JavaScript errors. Weekly laboratory runs help catch loading regressions. Good field thresholds are LCP ≤2.5 seconds, INP ≤200 milliseconds and CLS ≤0.1 at the 75th percentile; a synthetic navigation run cannot prove those field results. [Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)

Use current implementation for product claims. Verify any future earnings case with consent, date range, net results after stakes and fees, losses, sample context and limitations. Until that evidence exists, describe the rules and the possible match result. Never claim that players replace a salary or that a quoted receipt is profit.
